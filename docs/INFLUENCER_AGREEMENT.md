## 1. FTC Disclosure Requirements

You must comply with FTC Endorsement Guidelines (16 CFR Part 255) in all content promoting the product. These rules exist to prevent consumer deception — disclosures are about honesty, not red tape.

### Required disclosures

- Use **"Sponsored"**, **"Ad"**, **"Paid partnership"**, or **"X Ambassador"** — these are the clearest, simplest labels.
- Disclosures must be **visible without clicking "more"** — not buried, not in a hashtag cluster, not after a fold.
- If the endorsement is in **audio or video**, the relationship must be stated **verbally at the beginning** and **in writing at the beginning of the description**.

### Platform tools

- Use the platform's native paid partnership label where available (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube).
- If no native label exists, **superimpose the disclosure on the video** so it's visible throughout.
- Platform toggles alone are insufficient — disclosure must also appear in your caption or on-screen text.

### Affiliate links

- Any content containing an affiliate link requires disclosure **near or before** the link, every time.
- Acceptable: "paid link," "I earn a commission if you purchase," "I receive a commission."
- Not acceptable: "affiliate link" alone (consumers often don't know what it means), or disclosure only on a separate page.
- This applies to **Linktree, bio links, and any other link aggregation page** — disclose near each affiliate link.

### Honest reviews

- You must have **actually used the product** before making claims about it.
- If you don't like the product, you **cannot give a dishonest testimonial**. If the fit isn't right, we'd rather part ways than publish misrepresentations.
- You must use your **own ideas and words** to represent the product. We cannot script or direct your endorsement — doing so would require additional disclosure that the content was structured by the brand.
- Claims about the product must be **accurate and substantiated**. If a claim needs qualification, the qualification must appear alongside the claim in the same prominence.

### FTC resource

Full guidelines: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers
